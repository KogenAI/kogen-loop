# Stop a process group the moment it is gone

Slug: l-terminate-prompt-exit-latency
Model: not applicable — the stop keeps its states and transitions (SIGTERM to every group, absence polling up to the 5 s deadline, SIGKILL to the rest, waiting until no member can run); only the timing of the absence checks changes
Acceptance:
- .kogen/intents/l-terminate-prompt-exit-latency/acceptance.feature
- features/steps/l-terminate-prompt-exit-latency__stop.js

Today `sys.Terminate` (`sys/sys.go`) sends SIGTERM to each group and then sleeps 100 ms before it first checks with `kill(-pgid, 0)` whether a group is gone. It does this in up to 50 steps, then sends SIGKILL to the groups still alive. After SIGKILL it checks `groupOthers(g, 0)` at once and then every 20 ms until no group has a member that can still run. `sweepGroup` (the `Options.KillGroupOnExit` sweep after the leader ended) calls `Terminate` and then polls `kill(-pgid, 0)` for ESRCH at 20 ms steps, at most 50 times. So a group that exits on SIGTERM always costs at least 100 ms. That cost reaches every caller: `Session.Interrupt`, a done `RunContext` context, a fired `Options.Timeout`, `CapturedCommand.Stop`, the KillGroupOnExit sweep, `loop` and `cmd/kogen-loop`.

After this change, every stop checks for absence right after the signal and then polls with a growing step:

- **Terminate, SIGTERM phase.** Send SIGTERM to every group, as today. A group whose signal fails (ESRCH) is not kept. When no group is kept, `Terminate` returns at once: one failed signal per group, no other check and no sleep. Otherwise it checks every kept group with `kill(-g, 0)` immediately, in one pass over all of them. Then it sleeps 2 ms, 4 ms, 8 ms, 16 ms, 32 ms and 64 ms, and 100 ms each time after that, checking all remaining groups after each sleep. The step doubles and never exceeds the 100 ms used today. All groups share this one schedule: they are not stopped one after another. The SIGKILL deadline is 5 s after the SIGTERM pass. A sleep never runs past it (the last one is shortened), and at the deadline the groups still alive get SIGKILL.
- **Terminate, after SIGKILL.** Check `groupOthers(g, 0)` at once, as today. Then sleep 2, 4, 8 and 16 ms, and 20 ms each time after that (never more than today's 20 ms), until no group has a member that can still run. As today, a failed observation counts as presence, and this wait has no limit.
- **sweepGroup.** After `Terminate` returns, check `kill(-pgid, 0)` for ESRCH at once. Then sleep 2, 4, 8 and 16 ms, and 20 ms each time after that, for at most 1 s in total (today: 50 steps of 20 ms).

Unchanged: the 5 s escalation, SIGTERM and then SIGKILL in that order, the rule that `Terminate` does not return while a group member can still run, every `Result` (Status, Output, TimedOut), group disappearance and `finished`/`LiveGroups`/`Retire` bookkeeping. Also unchanged: `timeoutCustody`'s own 10 ms observation, `sweepOwnGroup` and the keeper in `sys/lifeline.go`, every event, tool text, the caller contract (`docs/contract/loop-v1.md`), options and exported signatures. The `Terminate` and `KillGroupOnExit` doc comments stay true as written. Use only the standard library. A small unexported helper shared by the three waits is fine.

Acceptance uses a Go collector kept as text (`DRIVER_SOURCE`, `//go:build ignore`) in `features/steps/l-terminate-prompt-exit-latency__stop.js`. The steps write it to a temporary file and `go run` it through `go -overlay` at the virtual path `features/steps/l-terminate-prompt-exit-latency__driver.go`. That path does not exist on disk and must not be created; nothing is written into the checkout. The collector uses only the exported `sys` API. Its groups are `/bin/sh -c` scripts started in their own process group with `Setpgid`, and the collector reaps their leaders in the background, as `RunContext` does. Its `Session.Run` cases run without the supervisor (the library default, because the collector never calls `sys.EnableSupervisor`). A supervised leader runs one `ps` listing of its own before it exits, so a supervised group does not end within 5 ms of SIGTERM. The collector reports, per case: the leaders' ends (`SIGTERM`, `SIGKILL`, `exit N`) and the live members left when the stop returned (one `ps -A -o pid=,pgid=,stat=` listing per group; zombie `Z`, dead `X` and exiting `E` rows do not count). It also reports each stop's duration in ms (for `timeout`, the time `Run` took beyond its 300 ms timeout) and the median. The step judges the median, so one scheduling hiccup on a loaded machine does not decide a case.

## Decisions
- A group that exits on SIGTERM ends custody promptly: check at once after the signal, then a short step that grows from a few milliseconds up to the current 100 ms (source: request)
- The step schedule is 2 ms doubling, capped at today's step of each wait: 100 ms after SIGTERM, 20 ms after SIGKILL and in the sweep. The SIGKILL deadline is 5 s after the SIGTERM pass and no sleep runs past it (source: request)
- Keep the 5 second SIGKILL escalation, the signals and their order, and the rule that custody is not handed back while a group member can still run. No change to any event, tool text or contract row (source: request)
- `sweepOwnGroup` and the keeper (the supervisor's own sweep when the caller dies) and `timeoutCustody`'s 10 ms observation are not among the stop paths the request names and stay unchanged (source: request)

## Source comparison
- Addressed, Problem: the first 100 ms sleep after SIGTERM, the sweep's 20 ms steps and the 20 ms steps after SIGKILL are replaced by an immediate check and the growing step above.
- Addressed, Need: immediate check, step growing up to 100 ms; escalation, signals, order and custody rule kept (scenarios "ignore" and "ignore-member", existing sys tests).
- Addressed, Synthetic acceptance 1 ("exits on SIGTERM within 5 ms ... under 50 ms, today about 100 ms"): case `prompt`, a group `sleep 30 >/dev/null & sleep 30 >/dev/null & echo ready; wait`, 5 runs, median under 50 ms. The same request applied to the stop of a command and a timed-out command: case `timeout` (`echo begun; sleep 30`, Timeout 300 ms, Status 143, Output `begun\n`, TimedOut true; under 50 ms beyond the timeout, about 106 to 129 ms today) and case `sweep` (`sleep 30 >/dev/null 2>&1 & echo started` with KillGroupOnExit, Status 0, Output `started\n`; whole Run under 80 ms, about 112 ms today).
- Addressed, Synthetic acceptance 2 ("ignore SIGTERM ... killed only after the 5 second escalation ... not handed back while a member runs"): case `ignore` (`trap '' TERM; sleep 30 >/dev/null & echo ready; wait`) and case `ignore-member` (`(trap '' TERM; echo ready; exec sleep 30 >/dev/null) & wait`, whose leader ends on SIGTERM). 4.5 s after the call began, `Terminate` has not returned and a member is live. It returns at least 5000 ms and under 6000 ms after the call, with no live member left.
- Addressed, Synthetic acceptance 3 ("several groups ... within the slowest group's time plus one short step"): case `several-prompt` (four prompt groups in one call, under 50 ms) and case `several-mixed` (two prompt groups and two groups `trap 'sleep 0.15; exit 0' TERM; echo ready; while :; do :; done` that end about 150 ms after SIGTERM). Their median is at least 150 ms and under 300 ms: the slowest group plus one step of at most 100 ms, with 50 ms slack. Stopping groups one after another takes at least 300 ms.
- Addressed, Synthetic acceptance 4 ("already gone costs one failed signal and no sleep"): case `gone`, a group whose leader already exited and was reaped (confirmed by ESRCH before the call), median under 1 ms. A sleep of the 2 ms first step fails it.
- Addressed, Synthetic acceptance 5 ("Results ... match the old behavior on every existing sys test"): the scenario hashes `contract/sys_test.go` and the nine `sys/*_test.go` files, which must stay byte-identical, and runs `go test -count=1 ./sys`. Every stop case also checks exact leaders, left members and Results, which the old code produces identically (observer bad control).
- Decided here, technical: the one schedule above for every group, the 5 s deadline measured from the SIGTERM pass instead of 50 counted sleeps, and the 1 s total bound of the sweep.
